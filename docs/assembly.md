# Assembly

[Back to the project](../README.md)

This guide assumes you have never built anything like it. Every hole of every printed part is shown in a picture with a red letter, and a table next to it says what goes into that hole, how many, with which tool, and in which step. Follow the steps in order: some parts cannot be reached once the next plate is on.

Allow an afternoon for the first build: about four hours, plus a night for the epoxy.

## Words used in this guide

- **Front** is the lens side, **rear** is the film side.
- **Photographer's left and right** are as you stand behind the camera looking at the ground glass. A picture taken from the front shows them mirrored: the photographer's left is on the right of the picture.
- **Insert** means a brass heat-set insert: a small threaded sleeve melted into the plastic so a metal screw has metal threads to bite. Sizes are written M3 x 3 (thread x length).
- **Proud** means sticking out above a surface; **flush** means level with it.
- The **Y plate** moves up and down (rise and fall). The **lens panel** (also called the X plate) moves left and right (shift) and carries the lens.

## Before you start

1. **Print and check the parts.** Everything in [printing](printing.md), including the checks under *After printing*: the four sliding faces must be flat.
2. **Do the test prints first.** Section 1 of [calibration](calibration.md) tells you whether your RB67 back fits before you spend a day assembling.
3. **Sort the hardware.** Put a strip of masking tape on the table and lay out the screws by length. Every step starts with a *You need* list.
4. **Clean the holes.** Pull out any stringing with a hook or a 3 mm drill turned by hand.

### Tools

| Tool | Used for |
|---|---|
| Soldering iron with heat-set tips for M3, M4 and 1/4" | inserts (a plain conical tip works for M3, badly for the bigger ones) |
| Hex keys 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 mm | grub screws, M3, M4 screws |
| M3 and M5 taps with a small tap wrench | threads cut into the plastic |
| 10 mm spanner (two) | M6 nuts |
| A small vice or a big clamp, a 6 mm bolt | pressing bushings, nuts and pads |
| Steel ruler or straightedge, caliper | flatness and heights |
| Feeler gauge 0.6 mm (or 6 sheets of 80 g printer paper) | ball plunger height |
| Sharp scissors, a new craft blade | velvet and felt |
| Loctite 243, two-part epoxy, isopropyl alcohol, cotton buds | as listed in the steps |

### Five techniques you will use

**1. Heat-set inserts.** This is most of the work, so practise on the shrinkage gauge first.

1. Set the iron to 230 C (ASA). Put the right tip on and let it heat for a minute.
2. Drop the insert into the hole with its **narrow end down**. It sits on the rim of the hole.
3. Put the tip into the insert and let it sink under its own weight plus a light push, straight down, for 5 to 10 seconds. Do not force it: if it does not move, wait.
4. Stop when the insert is 0.2 mm below the surface. Pull the tip straight out.
5. While the plastic is still soft, press a cold flat steel part (the side of a spanner) on the insert for five seconds: it comes out square and never proud.
6. Check: lay the straightedge across the hole. It must rock on nothing. An insert standing proud of a sliding face or of the back's seat will leak light or tilt the back.

The camera has 23 M3 inserts, 2 M4 and 4 of 1/4". The bigger ones take 15 to 20 seconds: go slowly and keep the iron vertical.

**2. Tapping a thread into the plastic.** Used for the two ball plungers (M5) and six small holes (M3). The holes are printed at the right size (4.2 and 2.5 mm).

1. Put the tap in the wrench and start it straight, looking from two sides.
2. Turn half a turn in, a quarter turn back, and repeat. The plastic chips must break off.
3. Go only as deep as the hole. Blow the chips out.

**3. Pressing.** Bushings, the knob nuts and the small pads are press fits. Use the vice or a clamp, never a hammer: ASA cracks. Put a flat piece of wood on the printed side.

**4. Threadlocker.** Loctite 243 only on metal-to-metal threads (the M6 nuts on the rods). Anaerobic threadlockers attack ASA and can crack it: keep them off the plastic, and never put them on a screw that goes into a printed part or an insert.

**5. Velvet and felt.** The camera has no bellows: the light is kept out by sliding faces 0.8 mm apart, with self-adhesive velvet in between. Four pieces are needed, V1 to V4.

1. Print the templates in `print/templates/` at 100 % scale. Measure the 100 mm bar on the sheet: if it is not 100 mm, fix the printer scaling first.
2. Tape the template on the back of the velvet (the paper side), and cut through both with scissors for the outer edge and a craft blade against a ruler for the window.
3. Measure the velvet thickness with the caliper, pressing lightly: it must be 0.9 to 1.2 mm. Thinner velvet does not touch the other plate; thicker velvet makes the movement stiff.
4. Degrease the plastic with alcohol. Peel off the backing, align one edge, and lay the velvet down from that edge like a screen protector. Press it all over with your thumb.
5. The velvet must never cover a hard pad, a ball plunger, or a hole a screw still has to reach.

> **MGN carriages lose their balls if they slide off the rail.** Keep each carriage on its rail, or on the plastic dummy rail it ships on, at all times. If one does come off, stop: the balls are 1 mm and they will roll everywhere.

## 3. Arca plates, top handle and levels

![Step 3](img/step_03.jpg)

**You need:** the two Arca plates with their 1/4" screws, the top handle, 2 M4 x 50 socket heads, 2 tubular vials, a few drops of epoxy.

![Top handle](img/parts/top_handle.jpg)

| | What goes here | Qty | How | Step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | tubular spirit vial | 1 | glued in the slot at the back of the bar | 3 |
| **B** | M4 x 50 socket head | 2 | from the top of the bar, down through the posts into the body inserts H | 3 |

1. **Arca plates.** Remove any rubber pad from the plates. Seat one plate in the bottom pocket and one in the side leg's pocket, and screw each in with two 1/4" screws into L and M. The plate's safety stops, if any, go toward the rear.
2. **Top handle.** Bolt it on with the two M4 x 50 screws into H. Tighten firmly: this handle carries the camera.
3. **Landscape level (A).** Put the camera on a tripod on its bottom plate and level it with a phone level on the top of the body. Put a drop of epoxy in the slot at the back of the handle bar, lay the vial in, and centre the bubble. Leave the camera alone until the epoxy sets.
4. **Portrait level (body J).** Move the camera to the side plate (lens pointing forward, the top of the body now toward the photographer's left), level it again with the phone, and glue the second vial in the slot J the same way.

A strap attaches to the top handle: loop Peak Design Anchors, or any cord, around the bar.

## 4. Vertical guide, bushings and body velvet

![Step 4](img/step_04.jpg)

**You need:** one 134 mm MGN9 rail, two MGN9H carriages, 2 M3 x 6 socket heads, 2 bronze bushings, epoxy, alcohol, velvet piece V1.

1. **Bond the rail.** Degrease the rail's underside and the channel floor with alcohol. Put a thin line of epoxy along the middle of the channel floor, between the two raised ribs. Press the rail in, pushed against the ribs, and fix it through its **two outermost holes** with the M3 x 6 screws into the inserts D. Wipe away any epoxy that squeezes out: it must not reach the rail's top.
2. **Carriages.** Hold a carriage's dummy rail end to end with the real rail and slide the carriage across. Do the same with the second. Leave both near the middle.
3. **Bushings (F).** Drop a bushing into the screw channel at its top end, then push it sideways into the round seat in the end wall until it is flush with the wall. Push it with a 6 mm bolt through its bore so it goes in square. Do the bottom one the same way. If a seat is tight, ream it with a 10 mm drill turned by hand.
4. **Velvet V1.** Cut V1 from its template. Stick it on the body front face around the window, the edge marked *rail side* toward the rail. The window of the velvet is 1 mm larger than the body's window all round. The strip G and the ball plunger E stay uncovered.

![Where the body velvet goes](img/parts/body_velvet.jpg)

**Check.** Slide each carriage the full length of the rail: smooth, no notch. Let the epoxy cure overnight before step 5.

| | What goes here | Qty | How | Step |

| **A** | stainless M6 nut | 1 | pressed into the hex pocket, flush | 5 |
| **B** | M3 x 4 nylon-tip grub screw | 1 | tap M3, it locks the knob on the rod | 5 |

1. **Inserts D.** Press the seven M3 inserts into the bottom channel of the Y plate front.
2. **Plunger E.** Tap M5 into the hole above the window and set the plunger 0.6 mm proud, as in step 1.
3. **Pads F.** Press the two printed pads into their pockets beside the plunger, with the vice, until they touch the bottom of the pocket. They stand 0.8 mm proud.
4. **Drive nut A.** Slide the brass nut into the turret on the plate rear, from the side, flats vertical. It is meant to float a little and cannot turn.
5. **Mount the plate.** Line the two carriages up with the plate's holes: they sit 42 mm apart, centre to centre. Lower the plate onto them with the turret going into the screw channel of the body. Fix it with the eight M3 x 12 screws B, from the front: all loose first, then tight in a cross pattern.
6. **Check the light seal faces.** The two pads C on the plate's rear must touch the body strip G, and the velvet V1 is squeezed between the faces. Push the plate by hand along its travel: it moves with an even, light drag.
7. **Plugs G.** Press the eight printed plugs into the counterbores, flush with the face. They keep the light seal continuous over the screw heads.
8. **Rise screw.** Push the 160 mm rod down from the top of the body through hole K, the top bushing, and the brass nut (turn the rod to thread it through the nut), then through the bottom bushing and out of the recess N in the bottom face.
9. **Bottom nuts (N).** Screw two M6 nuts onto the bottom end until the first touches the top of the recess. Put a small drop of Loctite 243 between them, away from the plastic, and jam them against each other with two spanners.
10. **Knob.** Press an M6 nut into the knob's hex pocket (A). Tap M3 into the small radial hole (B). Put the O-ring over the rod on the body's top face, screw the knob down the rod until the O-ring is lightly squeezed, and lock it with the grub screw.

**Check.** Turn the knob: the plate moves 1 mm per turn, clicks at zero, and stops hard at +25 and -25 mm. It stays where you leave it. If it is stiff, back the knob off a quarter turn before locking the grub screw again.

## 10. Film back

![Step 10](img/step_10.jpg)

1. **Loosen and lower.** Turn the wheel anticlockwise and slide the red blade down.
2. **Hook the bottom.** Hook the back's bottom lip under the fixed bottom rail of the Graflok module. This rail also releases the Pro-S dark slide interlock.
3. **Seat the back.** Swing the back onto the seat: its nose goes into the recess.
4. **Lock.** Slide the blade up so its two tongues enter the slots in the top of the back, then tighten the wheel.
5. **Dark slide.** It comes out toward the photographer's right, through the passage C of the body.
6. **Advance after each frame.** Press the back's own wind-stop release lever by hand: the camera has no automatic coupling (see [design notes](design.md#open-points)).

The ground glass goes on the same way. Now go to [calibration](calibration.md), then [using the camera](using.md).
