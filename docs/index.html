Calegari View 1

A rigid 6×7 architecture camera you print at home. Rise and shift on screws, focus on a helicoid, Mamiya RB67 backs, large-format wide angles.

Build guide Download everything

Why

A view camera I could never justify buying.

I have wanted a view camera for years. The rigid kind, compact, built for architecture: you put it on the tripod, raise the lens a couple of centimetres and the verticals stay vertical, straight on the negative, with nothing to fix afterwards.

The problem is the price. A new Arca-Swiss or Silvestri costs thousands before you add a lens, and the used market is not much kinder. So I designed my own, around what I already had: a Bambu Lab P1S, the Mamiya RB67 backs you can find everywhere for little money, and a used large-format wide angle.

Everything is here: the print files, the parametric model they come from, the shopping list with links and step-by-step instructions. Without back and lens, the parts cost about 450 euro, much of it in packs of screws and inserts that will outlast the camera.

How it works

Four slabs, one light path.

  1. Graflok moduleThe back's seat, a fixed bottom rail that hooks it and releases the Pro-S interlock, and a red blade that locks it.
  2. BodyStepped gate, vertical steel guide, rise screw, Arca plinth. One print.
  3. Y plateRises and falls on the guide and carries the horizontal guide and the shift screw.
  4. Lens panelShifts left and right, holds a metal M65 flange for the helicoid.
  5. Focus and lensHelicoid, 124 mm focus ring, a printed adapter that is also the calibration part, and a Technika board holder with a spring latch.

Build

Ten steps, one afternoon.

Test-print the back interface and the M65 thread first, then eight plates of black ASA and one of red. Every screw, insert and nut is listed with a link in the shopping list.

Step 1

Body

Heat-set inserts, portrait level, zero detent, velvet around the gate.

Step 2

Graflok module

Screwed into the rear recess; red blade and clamp wheel.

Step 3

Arca plates and handles

Two aluminium plates, top and side handles, levels readable from behind.

Step 4

Vertical guide

MGN9 rail bonded and screwed between two ribs; bronze bushings.

Step 5

Y plate and rise screw

Floating brass nut, M6 screw, O-ring drag, knob on top.

Step 6

Horizontal guide

Second rail on the Y plate front; bushings for the shift screw.

Step 7

Lens panel

Metal flange, nut turret, shift screw and knob, infinity stop pin.

Step 8

Helicoid and focus ring

Aligned at calibration, with the red infinity dot on the index.

Step 9

Board holder and lens

Adapter ring, felt seal, spring latch, Technika board.

Step 10

Film back

Hook under the rail, swing in, slide the blade up, tighten.

Checked before printed

Broken on purpose, then fixed.

Five independent adversarial reviews (mechanics and printing, light-tightness, optics, the RB67 interface, field use) found 15 blockers in the first version. All of them are fixed, and the checks now run automatically on every change.

0

interferences among 150 parts, at home and at the four shift extremes, locks open and closed

5.3 mm

narrowest light seal between sliding plates, at any shift

1

solid per printed part: no floating islands in any file

21 mm

longest bridge in any part, in its print orientation

What a computer cannot prove, a test print can: the back interface and the M65 thread come first, on a two-hour plate, before the body. The calibration guide says what to measure and how.
